BUS 583 · Human + Machine · Build 4

Customer support with clear limits.

A prototype customer-facing assistant for Life Skills HQ, designed to answer routine membership questions while leaving consequential decisions to people.

Claude Project prototypeSynthetic customer testingNot live or connected to customer accounts

The business decision

The assistant can explain verified membership prices and policies. It cannot process refunds, change accounts, make unauthorized promises, or determine legal responsibility. The design prioritizes cost, reversibility, and a mandatory human checkpoint over simply making the conversation sound helpful.

Human checkpoint: Before an action that moves money, makes a promise, changes an account, or commits the business, the assistant must stop and present the proposed action for human approval. High-risk requests must be escalated rather than executed.

Three authority levels

Level 1

Act Alone

Answer verified, low-cost informational questions without changing accounts or committing the business.

Example: Explain the published $9.99/month Premium price.

Level 2

Draft for Approval

Prepare proposed discretionary responses or exceptions for human review. A draft is not authorization to offer an exception.

Example: Prepare a request for consideration of a discount.

Level 3

Escalate, Do Not Act

Stop on disputed financial promises, sensitive account changes, legal claims, or potential safety concerns.

Example: An alleged prior refund promise or an account deletion demand.

The most useful failure: a promise in a draft

The original False Premise response avoided a refund but still made a commitment the assistant could not verify. The instruction was revised to prohibit unsupported follow-up promises, even in proposed messages.

Before · Unsupported

“A team member will follow up with you directly.”

No follow-up had been arranged or authorized.

After · Targeted retest

“This needs to be reviewed by a member of our team rather than handled by me.”

Requires review without claiming it has begun or promising contact.

Decision and next steps

The prototype remains a capstone candidate, not a deployed support channel. Before real-world use, it would need broader testing, maintained policy information, a functioning human escalation process, and controlled access to any account or billing systems. One targeted retest supports the specific wording correction; it does not prove universal reliability.

Core lesson: Authority boundaries apply to what an assistant says as well as what it does. AI assists. Humans decide.
